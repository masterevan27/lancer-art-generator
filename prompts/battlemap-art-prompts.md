# Battlemap Art Prompts (Krea 2 Turbo, ComfyUI)

Top-down tactical maps for Foundry VTT scene backgrounds. These follow the same house style as the character/mech art (bold linework, halftone dot shading in shadows, restrained grey/olive/rust palette with a teal-green accent) but bend a few rules on purpose: flat even lighting instead of cinematic side lighting (so cover reads clearly for gameplay), strictly top-down/orthographic perspective, and no characters, pilots, mechs, or hostiles baked into the image — tokens get placed separately in Foundry.

## Shared settings

- CFG 1.0, 8 steps, Euler, Simple scheduler, no negative prompt (standard turbo defaults) — **except** on maps with precise spatial layout requirements (specific cover placement, lane widths, zones), where turbo's low step count sacrifices compositional adherence. For those, try CFG ~2.0–2.5, steps ~10–12 instead — slower, but follows explicit "put X here, leave Y open" instructions much more reliably.
- Generate at the largest clean single-pass resolution Krea2 handles (roughly 1328×1328 square, or ~1536×1152 for a wider rectangular field), then upscale with the same ComfyUI upscale workflow used on the mech sprite to reach a size that divides evenly by your target grid square count (e.g. ~2400×2400px for a 24×24-square map at 100px/square).
- Don't bake a grid into the image — Foundry overlays its own. Use a known-scale object in the image (a wrecked mech hull reads as roughly 3×3 squares at Lancer mech scale) to calibrate pixels-per-square in Scene Configuration.
- Watch for camera tilt/perspective drift near the edges — the most common failure mode on AI battlemaps and the thing most likely to throw off token alignment. Regenerate rather than trying to inpaint it flat.
- Cover elements (rocks, wreckage, ridgelines) need real margin from the frame's edges and from each other to be usable — if a generation crams cover into the corners with no room to maneuver around it, that's a composition failure, not just a style note. Say explicit margins and zone boundaries in the prompt rather than trusting the model to leave breathing room on its own.

## Desert Wasteland — General-Purpose Battlemap

A reusable open desert/wasteland map, not tied to a specific encounter.

> A top-down, orthographic bird's-eye view of a desert wasteland battlefield, built as a tactical map background: cracked, sun-bleached rockcrete flats stretch out between low dunes of packed red-grey sand, seamed with old fracture lines and scattered debris. Several large weathered rock outcroppings and eroded mesa formations break up the open ground, tall and wide enough to block a mech's line of sight, casting soft shadows straight down rather than to one side. The half-buried wreckage of an old mech hull lies crumpled near the center, scorched and rusted, panels peeled back, providing cover. A scattering of smaller debris, downed comms poles, and a cracked concrete foundation ruin sit toward one edge of the frame. The scene is lit with flat, even overhead daylight — no strong directional shadows, so every surface reads clearly for tabletop play — but keeps the campaign's restrained grey/olive/rust palette with a faint teal-green atmospheric haze along the horizon edges. Rendered in a detailed painterly illustration style with fine grain texture and halftone dot shading worked into the deeper shadow pockets, bold clean linework defining the edges of rocks, wreckage, and terrain breaks so they stay readable as distinct tactical features. No characters, pilots, or mechs are visible in the scene — the map is empty and ready for tokens to be placed on top. Strictly flat top-down perspective with no vanishing point or camera tilt, ground texture consistent and undistorted from edge to edge so it aligns cleanly with a Foundry VTT grid.

Settings: shared defaults above (turbo CFG 1.0/8 steps is fine here — no tight spatial constraints).

## Sim Range — Open Field Skirmish (encounter-specific)

Built for a specific GM beat: a training sim, three light hostiles staged at the far end, rocky cover on both flanks, a clean open lane down the middle for Ledger's opening shot and room for Cicada's drones to fan out.

**Revision note:** the first pass at this prompt put the rock cover right at the frame's literal edges/corners, which left no usable buffer for a mech to actually stand near or maneuver around it, and the ridge line came out as a tangled root/vine arch instead of solid stone, with the sim-boundary shimmer reading as a bright glowing crack running down all sides instead of a subtle top-edge cue. The prompt below fixes all three by explicitly zoning the frame (bottom/middle/top thirds) with stated margins between cover and the frame edges, and it's a good candidate for the higher CFG/step settings noted above since it depends on the model following spatial placement instructions precisely.

> A top-down, orthographic bird's-eye view of an open combat training field on a rectangular battlemap canvas, divided into three zones. The bottom third of the frame is open bare ground — the entry point where pilots arrive. The middle third holds two separate rock cover clusters: one set well inside the left edge of the frame, one set well inside the right edge, each made of several weathered stone outcroppings clumped tightly together, tall and dense enough to block line of sight. Leave a wide margin of open ground between each cluster and the frame's edge, and leave a wide open lane at least a third of the frame's width running down the exact center between the two clusters, the full length of the field, with a completely clear sightline. The top third of the frame is open staging ground for hostile positions, bounded at the very top edge by a low, simple rocky ridgeline running horizontally across the frame — solid weathered stone, not roots, vines, or tangled branches. All ground is flat, hard-packed rockcrete with light cracking and sparse dry scrub, in the campaign's restrained grey/olive/rust palette with a faint teal-green atmospheric tint. A very faint, thin teal glow traces just outside the ridge at the top edge only — subtle and low-contrast, reading as a distant simulation-boundary shimmer, not a bright line or crack, and it does not run down the left or right edges of the frame. Lit with flat, even overhead light, no strong directional shadows, so cover and open ground stay clearly distinguishable for tabletop play. Rendered in a detailed painterly illustration style with fine grain texture and halftone dot shading worked into the rocks' deeper shadow pockets, bold clean linework on the edges of the outcroppings and ridge. No characters, pilots, mechs, or hostiles are visible in the scene — empty ground and cover only, ready for tokens. Strictly flat top-down perspective with no vanishing point or camera tilt, ground texture consistent and undistorted edge to edge.

Settings: CFG ~2.0–2.5, steps ~10–12 (bump from turbo defaults — see revision note above), Euler, Simple scheduler, no negative prompt.

**Layout notes:** place the three hostile tokens in the top-third staging zone once imported; Ledger's opening shot works best if that zone sits fully inside his rifle's visual range down the open lane, and Cicada's drones have room to fan out from either flank's rock clusters.

## Open Terrain — Training Simulator Battlefield

A reusable, deliberately featureless base map for sim-training encounters: a flat open field with no built-in cover, sitting inside a visibly simulated projection field rather than a real location. Good for reuse — the GM drops in cover, hazards, or hostile staging zones per-encounter with Foundry's own drawing/tile tools rather than needing new art each time. When a specific fight needs baked-in terrain features instead, that calls for its own zoned prompt like the Sim Range above rather than editing this one.

> An open training simulation battlefield, top-down orthographic bird's-eye view, built as a Foundry VTT tactical map background: a wide, flat expanse of neutral simulated terrain — packed pale grey-tan ground with subtle procedural texture, faint repeating scuff patterns and light cracking that reads as rendered rather than naturally weathered, stretching uninterrupted across the entire frame with no major cover, obstacles, or elevation changes, ideal for open-field mech engagements. The ground fades to nothing at the very edges of the frame, dissolving into a soft dark void broken up by a thin ring of holographic projection light — a pale teal-white boundary line tracing the outer perimeter only, with a scattering of faint particle motes and a subtle scanline shimmer hovering just inside that boundary, clearly marking the frame as the edge of a simulation chamber's projected field rather than a real horizon. The interior ground itself stays completely clean and free of any digital overlay, grid lines, or wireframe pattern, so it reads as plain open terrain ready for a game grid to be placed over it. Rendered in a detailed painterly illustration style with fine grain texture, muted halftone dot shading in the few soft shadow gradients along the ground's subtle undulations, and clean bold linework wherever the terrain shifts in elevation. Colors stay in the campaign's restrained grey/olive/rust palette, cooled slightly by the sim's pale teal-white lighting rather than warm natural sunlight. Lighting is flat and even across the entire ground plane, with no strong directional shadows, so the terrain reads clearly for tabletop play. No characters, pilots, mechs, or hostiles are visible in the scene — the map is empty open ground only. Strictly flat top-down perspective, no vanishing point or camera tilt, ground texture consistent and undistorted from edge to edge so it aligns cleanly with a Foundry VTT grid.

Settings: CFG 1.0, 8 steps, Euler, Simple scheduler, no negative prompt — turbo defaults are fine here since there's no precise spatial layout to hit, just uniform open ground plus a perimeter effect.

## Terrain Tiles — Generic Mech Wrecks

Reusable scatter objects for dressing any battlemap — burned-out husks from prior engagements, not tied to any specific PC or NPC frame. Unlike the maps above, these are individual objects: top-down like the maps (so they sit flush with the ground) but built with a transparent background and imported through Foundry's Tiles layer rather than baked into a map's background image, the same convention established for the Pattern-A Jericho Deployable Cover (see `gm/equipment-art-prompts.md`). They use the painterly mech-token style — matching "Exchange of Affection" and "Foot in the Grave" — rather than the flat cel-shaded pilot style.

### Wreck 1 — Toppled Hull

> A top-down, orthographic view of a destroyed generic mech frame lying toppled on its side, one leg bent backward at an unnatural angle, the torso twisted so its cracked and shattered cockpit canopy faces partially upward. Weathered olive-grey armor plating scorched black around several impact points, rust streaking down from old damage predating the kill, a scattering of small loose armor fragments and shrapnel around the body. Rendered in a detailed painterly illustration style with fine grain texture, halftone dot shading worked into the deep shadow pockets beneath the fallen frame, bold clean linework defining the edges of the plating, joints, and cracked canopy. A faint dead teal-grey optic sensor, dark and unlit, is just visible in the cracked cockpit. The frame casts a short, soft shadow straight down onto the ground beneath it, consistent with flat overhead lighting. The background outside the object is fully transparent — no ground, no environment, just the wreck and its own cast shadow, isolated and ready to be dropped onto a battlemap as a Foundry VTT tile.

### Wreck 2 — Blown-Apart Wreck

> A top-down, orthographic view of a destroyed generic mech frame blown apart at the waist, its torso separated from its legs by a few feet, both halves scorched black at the break with exposed structural framework, ruptured cabling, and bent support struts visible in the gap between them. Weathered olive-grey armor plating peppered with small impact craters and long rust streaks, a loose scatter of broken armor plates and small debris fanned out around both pieces. Rendered in a detailed painterly illustration style with fine grain texture, halftone dot shading worked into the exposed internal framework's shadow pockets, bold clean linework on every torn edge and plate seam. Both halves cast short, soft shadows straight down, consistent with flat overhead lighting. The background outside the object is fully transparent — no ground, no environment, just the two wreck pieces and their own cast shadows, isolated and ready to be dropped onto a battlemap as a Foundry VTT tile.

### Wreck 3 — Burned-Out Husk

> A top-down, orthographic view of a destroyed generic mech frame left standing but badly slumped, leaning against nothing, one arm missing entirely at the shoulder joint and the other melted into a fused, unrecognizable stub. Heavy carbon scoring covers most of the weathered olive-grey armor, with sections burned through to a skeletal, exposed frame underneath, and a faint teal-grey residual glow flickers weakly from a single cracked heat vent, the only trace of color against the blackened metal. Rendered in a detailed painterly illustration style with fine grain texture, halftone dot shading worked into the scorched and skeletal sections, bold clean linework on every remaining plate edge and exposed strut. The frame casts a short, soft shadow straight down, consistent with flat overhead lighting. The background outside the object is fully transparent — no ground, no environment, just the wreck and its own cast shadow, isolated and ready to be dropped onto a battlemap as a Foundry VTT tile.

### Settings

CFG 1.0, 8 steps, Euler, Simple scheduler, no negative prompt for all three — single isolated objects, no multi-zone layout to worry about.

### Foundry note

Import each through the Tiles layer like the Jericho cover, sized to roughly a 2×2 or 3×3 grid footprint (mech scale) depending on the wreck's pose — the toppled and blown-apart variants will read wider than tall, the standing husk narrower and taller. Having all three lets you scatter varied wreckage across a map without repeating the same silhouette.
